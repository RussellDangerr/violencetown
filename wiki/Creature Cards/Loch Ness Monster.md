# Loch Ness Monster

> *"There's something down here bigger than the pipes."*

## Description

A colossal sea serpent wedged into the Violencetown sewer system. Her body is a mythological chimera: the sinuous length of a Chinese lung dragon, the scaled ridgeback and sea-weathered hide of a Norse serpent, and the long-necked silhouette of the classic Loch Ness photograph. She is ancient, patient, and currently — technically — stuck.

Violencians call her **Lonny**.

She entered the sewers through a drainage channel connected to the harbor sometime before anyone can remember. She fit when the pipes were new. The city grew around her. The pipes narrowed. Now she can move through certain corridors but not others, her massive coils filling tunnels floor-to-ceiling, her body threading through the sewer network like a snake through a chain-link fence. She's not trapped — she's wedged. There's a difference, and she'll tell you about it.

## Folklore Origin

The Loch Ness Monster is Scotland's most famous cryptid — a lake-dwelling creature first reported in the 6th century, popularized by the 1934 "surgeon's photograph." The traditional image is a plesiosaur silhouette: long neck, small head, humped body.

Lonny's design pushes past the plesiosaur cliché by fusing three mythological serpent traditions:

- **Chinese lung dragon** — The long, serpentine body without wings. Lung dragons are water-associated, wise, and benevolent in Chinese mythology. Lonny's temperament borrows from this tradition: she's not hostile, she's inconvenienced.
- **Norse Jörmungandr** — The world-serpent whose body encircles Midgard. Lonny doesn't encircle the world, but she does fill the sewer system's main arteries. Her body IS the infrastructure problem. When she shifts, pipes burst. When she breathes, the water level changes.
- **Classic Nessie** — The long neck, the humped back breaking the surface, the blurry photograph. The parts of Lonny the player sees first — a neck disappearing around a corner, a hump rising above the sludge — are pure Loch Ness iconography.

The result: a creature that reads as "Nessie" at a distance but reveals itself as something far older and stranger up close.

## Appearance

**Scale:** Lonny is the largest creature in Violencetown. Her full body spans multiple map screens. The player never sees all of her at once — only segments: a coil blocking a corridor, a scaled flank pressed against a tunnel wall, a neck stretching around a corner, a tail tip disappearing into a drain.

**Body:** Long, serpentine, no limbs. Scaled hide in deep blue-green and tarnished bronze, barnacle-crusted in patches from centuries in salt and sludge. Faintly bioluminescent along the ventral ridge — a soft teal glow that illuminates the sewer water around her. Dorsal spines run her full length, flattened from pressing against pipe ceilings.

**Head:** Small relative to body (Nessie proportions). Large dark eyes, not threatening — curious, tired, amused. A long snout with whisker-like barbels (lung dragon influence). When she speaks, her jaw barely moves — the voice seems to come from the water itself.

**Pixel art notes:** The player encounters Lonny as **tile-spanning segments**. A Lonny coil is a 3-4 tile wide barrier of scaled blue-green blocking a corridor. Her neck, if visible, is a 1-2 tile wide serpentine shape stretching across multiple screens. The bioluminescent glow is the key visual identifier — before you see Lonny, you see teal light reflecting off the sewer water.

## Game Role

**Tier: Unique.** There is one Lonny. She is not a combat encounter. She is not an enemy type. She is a **world feature** — a living piece of the Sewer's geography.

### Phase 1: The Body (Early Sewer)

The player encounters Lonny's body before they encounter Lonny. Massive coils block corridors. Scaled walls that aren't walls — they breathe, slowly. Sludge flows differently around her (displaced by her bulk). The player may initially read these as environmental features: "That's a weird wall texture." Then the wall moves.

- Lonny's body segments are **impassable terrain** that shift on long timers (every 50-100 ticks). A corridor blocked by a coil now may open later — and one that was open may close.
- Sewer water levels fluctuate when Lonny breathes. Sludge pools appear and drain in rhythmic cycles.
- The [[Wererat]] NPCs know about Lonny and treat her as infrastructure: "Don't go through the east pipe, Lonny's blocking it again." They're annoyed, not afraid.
- First-time players won't know what they're seeing. The journal entry: "Something massive is down here. The walls moved."

### Phase 2: The Encounter (Mid Sewer)

Deep in the sewer, past the soap mines and the [[Fungus King]]'s territory, the player rounds a corner and finds Lonny's head. She's resting in a flooded junction room, her neck draped across the chamber, her eyes half-closed. She's been here a long time.

First dialogue: she's dry. Not hostile-dry — conversationally dry. She knows she's stuck. She's been stuck for longer than Violencetown has had a name. She's fine with it, mostly. The sludge is terrible for her scales. The wererats are noisy neighbors. But she can't leave, and she's made peace with that.

She does NOT ask for help immediately. She's too proud and too old for that. The quest to free her is something the player discovers is possible — not something she requests.

### Phase 3: The Unsticking (Late-game / Episode quest)

Helping Lonny get unstuck is a **multi-zone quest** that requires:

1. **Understanding the problem.** Lonny's body threads through the entire sewer pipe network. She can't back out (too many turns) and can't go forward (pipes narrow). She needs a wider exit — which means either breaking infrastructure or finding the original harbor drain she entered through.
2. **The harbor drain.** Connected to [[Street]] / the boardwalk. It's been sealed, built over, or incorporated into Bank Street's foundations. Opening it may require confronting [[The Financier]]'s territory — the vampires built on top of Lonny's exit.
3. **Sludge reduction.** The sludge has calcified around parts of her body, cementing her in place. Clearing the sludge (soap, water, the [[Sewer]] cleansing mechanics) frees her segment by segment.
4. **Lonny's cooperation.** She has to WANT to move. Her pride, her exhaustion, and her attachment to the sewer (it's been home for centuries) all need to be addressed — through dialogue, gifts ([[Disposition]] system), or by improving the sewer conditions enough that she has a reason to believe the outside is better.

**Reward:** Lonny leaving the sewer fundamentally changes the zone. Corridors that were blocked for the entire game open permanently. Water levels stabilize. The sewer becomes navigable in ways it never was. And Lonny — freed — becomes an overworld presence, glimpsed in the harbor water off the boardwalk, a massive shape moving beneath the surface. She doesn't forget who helped her.

## Relationships

- **[[Wererats]]** — Tolerate her. She's been here longer than them. They route around her body like traffic around a parked truck. Some younger wererats don't believe she's alive — they think the "walls" are just old pipes.
- **[[Carrion]]** — Respects Lonny. Both are ancient, both are stuck (Carrion in the south corridor, Lonny in the pipe network). They may have history. Carrion might be the one who tells the player Lonny's name.
- **[[Texas Beholdem]]** — Lonny's body forms part of the boundary of Beholdem's territory. He doesn't mess with her. Even a cowboy beholder knows not to poke a sleeping sea serpent.
- **[[The Financier]]** — The vampires sealed the harbor drain. Whether they know Lonny is down there is an open question. If they do, they trapped her deliberately (keeping a powerful neutral off the board). If they don't, it's collateral damage from their infrastructure projects — another thing the vampires broke without knowing.
- **[[Jersey]]** — As the [[Jersey Devil]], Jersey may know about Lonny. Two ancient cryptids in the same city. Their relationship (allies? strangers? old rivals?) is unwritten but load-bearing for the mid-game reveal.
- **[[Behest]]** — The Library may have records of Lonny's arrival. Old harbor maps, drainage schematics, sailor testimonies. Behest would know where these documents are filed. "Section 597.3 — Aquatic Fauna, Subterranean. I believe there's a file."

## Design Notes

- Lonny is the **first cryptid outside the [[Circus]]**. This breaks the pattern that all cryptids are carnival-aligned and establishes that cryptids are scattered across Violencetown — the Circus is where they congregate, not where they're confined. Future cryptids can appear in any zone.
- Lonny is a **world-feature NPC**, not an enemy or a party member. She changes the map geometry. Her body is terrain. Her breathing affects water levels. She's less like an NPC and more like a living dungeon element.
- The "stuck" premise is pure KND energy: a mythological sea serpent dealing with a zoning and infrastructure problem. She's not cursed, she's not imprisoned, she's not in danger. She just can't fit through the pipes anymore. It's bureaucratic, it's mundane, and it's applied to something extraordinary.
- Lonny's quest is an **episode** in the Adventure Time sense — a self-contained creative bet that uses the game's existing systems (sludge cleansing, disposition, multi-zone traversal) to tell a story that's never been in a videogame before. "Help a sea serpent who's too big for the plumbing" is the one-new-thing-per-episode rule in action.
- Her visual design (Chinese + Norse + Nessie) makes her feel mythologically deep without being region-locked. She's not "just Scottish Nessie" — she's something older that Nessie is a shadow of. Violencetown's Loch Ness Monster predates Loch Ness.

## Open Questions

- **Can Lonny fight?** If attacked, does she retaliate? She's massive — a tail swipe could clear a room. But fighting Lonny should feel like a terrible idea, not a challenge. She's not a boss. She's a neighbor.
- **Lonny's relationship to sludge.** Is sludge hurting her? Or is she immune / adapted? If sludge is harming her, that's additional motivation for the player to clean the Sewer. If she's adapted, the sludge calcification is just uncomfortable, not dangerous.
- **Does Lonny have a Creature Card?** Yes — but it starts nearly blank. The player's card reads "???" until they've seen enough segments, heard enough wererat rumors, and finally met her face-to-face. The card fills in progressively across the phases.
- **Lonny and the harbor post-unsticking.** If freed, does Lonny leave Violencetown entirely? Or does she stay in the harbor, becoming a new overworld presence? Recommendation: she stays. She likes it here. She just needed more room.
